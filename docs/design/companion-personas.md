# Your AI Companion — communication preferences

## Design decision

One HSBC AI Companion, four ways of communicating. These are support styles, not separate advisers with different knowledge, investment attitudes or permissions. The customer chooses a style and initiative separately. All styles remain respectful and warm.

| Style | Emphasis | Example approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guide | Balanced perspective | Explain the situation and offer a choice. |
| Listener | Reflection and emotional space | Start with understanding; invite the customer's perspective without assuming how they feel. |
| Analyst | Numbers and reasoning | Show the relevant figure, distinctions and assumptions. |
| Coach | Manageable action | Suggest a concrete next step, leaving the decision with the customer. |

The four abstract marks keep the shared HSBC sparkle. Shape as well as colour distinguishes them. They are not human avatars or named relationship managers.

## Initiative is independent of style

- **When I ask:** ordinary reading-context suggestions become a stable availability message. Opening it retrieves the actual reading context. No arrival thinking animation. Payment conditions, important account updates and human messages remain visible.
- **In useful moments:** existing contextual behaviour, following what the customer is viewing.
- **Suggest a next step:** the existing contextual action becomes the headline when an actionable next step exists. No extra notifications, timers or repeated reminders.

This changes the in-app Companion only. It does not suppress the main application’s discovery cards, goals or mandatory financial information. It does not grant automation authority.

## Explainable recommendation, explicit choice

The highest supported Money Personality trait suggests a starting style: Planning/Patience → Analyst, Rhythm/Focus → Coach, Generosity → Listener, Spontaneity → Guide. An unformed portrait gets a neutral Guide default.

This mapping is a design hypothesis, **not a validated psychological matching model**. Money behaviour does not establish a communication preference. The interface explains that limitation in “Why this suggestion?”. Recommendation never changes the saved style. Balance, wealth, HSBC tier and private household-member information do not influence it. Feedback and user research should test these hypotheses before a production recommendation model is considered.

A draft lets customers compare styles with the same scenario facts across Now, You and Future. Back discards changes. Save applies across the app and persists on this device, per scenario. Restore starting style returns to the scenario’s initial style and is previewed before Save. Resetting the scenario resets the preferences. A communication preference does not create a financial receipt, points or an artificial relationship milestone.

## Money Personality refinement

The earlier wealth-personality reference contributes the structure, not its investment recommendations:

- Strengths: how a dominant habit might help.
- Possible blind spots: how that same strength might become unhelpful in another context.
- Things to think about: one practical, open reflection, with a route to add the customer's own perspective.

These sit in **Explore your portrait**, alongside interpretations and correction. **Behind your portrait** retains measured evidence. Unformed portraits do not receive invented strengths or weaknesses; shared portraits use their own traits; the household view is not assigned one person's blind spots. Reflection is neither a diagnosis nor an investment-risk classification.

## Integration and prototype scope

`companion/model.mjs` owns preference validation, the recommendation and communication policy. `support/model.mjs` applies the policy once, after building the existing contextual evidence. The actual card and conversation use the chosen abstract avatar. Ordinary scripted chat replies use the same tone policy. Human replies, escalation, approval flows and account attention copy remain unchanged.

The preview uses the actual support model and scenario data. The prototype demonstrates curated styles and deterministic wording, not a live language model or a newly trained memory system. More specialised conversations retain their existing authored response and critical facts; the shared wrapper adjusts conversational framing. No financial quantities are generated by the persona.

## Research informing the design

- [Google PAIR — Feedback + Control](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/feedback-controls/): make feedback consequential and understandable; let customers see the effect of explicit controls; do not infer preference from an ambiguous action.
- [Google PAIR — Mental Models](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/mental-models/): establish appropriate expectations about adaptation and avoid implying human understanding beyond the system's capability.
- [Microsoft Research — Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction-eighteen-best-practices-for-human-centered-ai-design/): support dismissal and correction, provide global controls, adapt cautiously and make context relevant.

These sources support the interaction principles. They do not establish that the four styles or trait-to-style mapping are empirically validated. The next research step is to compare customer-selected styles with recommendations, assess comprehension and perceived control, and check whether initiative remains useful over repeated visits.

## Scenario starting styles and editorial portrait

Each demonstration begins with a different communication preference: Alex → Guide (a familiar, balanced introduction); Jordan → Coach (encouragement and a manageable next step); Sam → Listener (space for family priorities and reflection); Elena → Analyst (detail and reasoning). Jordan begins with “Suggest a next step”; the other scenarios use “In useful moments”. These are authored scenario preferences, separate from the optional trait-based suggestion. Saved choices always take precedence, and changing the portrait does not silently change the Companion.

The You-tab entry uses the same page gutters as the Journey card. Explore your portrait now reads as one editorial article: personal cover and artwork → strengths → interpreted patterns → the other side of a strength → an open reflection → confirmation and measured evidence. Trait shapes connect the artwork to the reading. The interpreted patterns retain their illustrated, softly tinted tiles, with a trait symbol and a route into the evidence. The surrounding strengths and reflection use typography, rules and space; one quiet inset marks the closing question. Existing observations, customer corrections and contextual Companion topics remain connected.
