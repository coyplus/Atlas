# Interface copy principles

Every visible sentence must help the customer understand a fact or status, make a decision, or take an action. If removing it changes none of those, remove it.

- Lead with the amount, status, requirement or decision. Do not preface it with motivational framing.
- Name the action and its destination. Prefer “Review budget” to “Explore the idea”.
- Avoid repeating the screen title in an eyebrow, card title and footer. State context once where possible.
- Avoid default reassurance such as “Take your time”, “The choice is yours”, “Here to help” and “Your next step”. Explain the actual control or consequence when it matters.
- Keep useful reassurance specific: pausing automation does not cancel a manual obligation; a review does not move money; a connection is read-only. These explain behaviour and must survive copy cuts.
- Keep amounts, dates, rates, conditions, consent, risk and recovery consequences clear. Concision must not hide an important qualification.
- Keep source-authoring and implementation notes in the documentation. The demo presentation identifies illustrative data; each product component should not repeat a prototype disclaimer.
- Give secondary detail a sheet. The summary should expose the current benefit or status and an explicit route to the detail.
- Use gestures and icons consistently, with accessible names and a visible alternative. Explain a non-obvious gesture once in the relevant context rather than on every page.

Apply this standard to Stories, AI cards, account/pot summaries, agreements, insights, journeys and new components. Human support can remain warm: empathy should address the customer’s situation, rather than add a generic slogan.
